# Philosophy

VSlices is not only a set of tools for building software.

It is a way of thinking about how systems are discovered, documented, designed, implemented, and evolved without losing their original intent.

This section explains the ideas that support the suite.

The goal is not to define strict rules or universal answers. The goal is to make explicit the principles that guide VSlices when deciding what to document, what to design, what to implement, and what complexity to avoid until the domain truly needs it.

## Read this section when

Read this section if you want to understand:

* why VSlices exists
* what software engineering problems it tries to reduce
* how documentation, design, architecture, and implementation relate to each other
* why VSlices prefers progressive complexity over premature architecture
* why preserving intent matters throughout the complete lifetime of a system

## What belongs here

This section should contain foundational ideas that affect the whole suite.

A document belongs here when it explains a belief, principle, or long-term concern shared by VSlices Framework, VSlices Design, VSlices Docs Standard, and VSlices Method.

Examples:

* continuity of knowledge
* progressive complexity
* documentation as living design
* architecture without dogma
* explicit behavior
* preservation of domain intent
* software evolution without loss of meaning

## What does not belong here

This section should not contain product-specific guides, implementation details, templates, or technical specifications.

Those belong in each product section.

Use this section to explain why VSlices thinks the way it thinks.

Use the product sections to explain how that philosophy becomes concrete.
